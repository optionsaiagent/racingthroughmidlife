We are not going to tell you how many hours to sleep. We do not know your life. We will tell you what the full-distance decision did to ours.

## The line we keep repeating

The full distance is not a personality. It is a season you have to fund with sleep.

We wrote that when we were deciding whether to go 140.6, and we have not found a better way to say it. Every long-course season we have done since has been paid for the same way: earlier bedtimes, dropped evenings, a social calendar that got thin for a while. Not forever. For a season.

## What changed after fifty

At thirty a hard week and a bad night could coexist. Now they cannot. A long ride on five hours of sleep is not a long ride. It is a long ride plus two days of feeling wrong, and the two days are where the next workout was supposed to be.

We noticed it most in the mornings. The 5 a.m. alarm is the honest measurement. When it goes off and the body says yes, the week is funded. When it goes off and the body says something else, the smart move is to listen, take the easy day, and keep the season.

## What we actually did

- **Bedtime first.** The workout is at dawn because of the heat, so the bedtime is early because of the workout. We stopped negotiating the bedtime. It is the one line item that pays for everything else.
- **Easy weeks in ink.** The easy week goes on the calendar when the block is planned, not when we feel like we need it. By the time we feel like we need it, we have needed it for a week.
- **Real recovery after the big days.** The week after a marathon or a long-course race is a rest week. Not a light week. Rest. Walk, swim easy, sleep. The [week after the marathon](/notes/the-week-after-the-marathon) note has the specifics.

## The season ends

One more thing about funding a season with sleep: seasons end. That is not a failure. The full distance is not who we are. It is a thing we do for a few months, and then we go back to being two people who race the local calendar and sleep like normal humans. Knowing the end exists makes the middle survivable.
