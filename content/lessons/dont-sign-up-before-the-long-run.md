The internet will tell you to sign up first so you can't back out. We did exactly that, and it worked, and we'd still tell you to be careful with it.

## How it went for us

Michelle watched the 2021 Honolulu Marathon from the sidewalk and said we should run it the next year for Jay's fiftieth. A couple of days later, when registration opened for locals, we signed up. At that point the longest either of us had ever run was 8.1 miles, we'd done a 5K or 10K here and there without enjoying them, and both of us had spent years in the Army hating every mile the Army made us run. The date did its job. It turned a marathon into something that existed on a calendar instead of something Jay had decided he'd never want.

The date wasn't the plan, though. We started in January with 1.5 miles one day and 2 the next, walking three days a week to break it up, with no real idea what we were doing. The long run went past ten, then to a [half marathon at Hibiscus](/races/hibiscus-half-2022) in May, then past the half through a Honolulu summer, then through the readiness series to a 30K in October. The marathon in December was only real because of that. If the half had gone badly, we'd have moved the marathon to the next year and been fine with it, because nobody was keeping score on a fiftieth birthday except the guy having it.

## Bridges, not leaps

Every distance since has followed the same shape. The half was the bridge to the marathon. The Olympic tri in May 2023 was the bridge to the 70.3 two weeks later. The 112-mile Dick Evans ride in August was the bridge to the full-distance bike leg, and the standalone marathon was the bridge to the run at the end of the full. A bridge is a race you can already picture finishing, and it tells you the truth about the next one before the next one can hurt you.

## Why this matters more after 40

At 30 you can bluff a distance, be sore, and finish. In your forties and fifties the bluff has consequences that last weeks. A body that hasn't done the long run doesn't have the tendons, the eating habits, or the pacing sense to survive a bad day at a long distance, and the bib doesn't give you any of that. Only the long run does. So sign up if the date is what gets you moving, and then treat the long run as the thing you actually owe the race.
