The internet will tell you to sign up first so you cannot back out. Sometimes that works. Sometimes it puts a person who has never run 13 miles into a 26.2 they will remember as a medical event.

Our version is different, and it is the only reason the marathon happened.

## The date and the work are two different things

Michelle watched the 2021 Honolulu Marathon and said we should run it the next year for Jay's fiftieth. That was the date. It did its job: it made a marathon a thing that existed in the future instead of a thing Jay had decided he would never want.

But the date was not the plan. The longest run in the house was 8.2 miles. A marathon was not real yet. It became real in stages: a long run that went past ten, then a half marathon at Hibiscus in May, then long runs that went past the half, then December.

We paid for the marathon when the half was done. Not before. If the half had gone badly, the marathon would have moved to the next year, and we would have been fine with that. Nobody is keeping score on a fiftieth birthday except the person having it.

## Bridges, not leaps

Every distance we have raced since then followed the same shape. The half was the bridge to the marathon. The Olympic triathlon in May 2023 was the bridge to the 70.3 three weeks later. The 112-mile Dick Evans ride in August was the bridge to the full-distance bike leg. The standalone marathon was the bridge to the run at the end of the full.

The bridge is a race you can already picture finishing. It tells you the truth about the next one before you have paid for it.

## Why this matters more after 45

At 30 you can bluff a distance. You will be sore and you will finish. At 50 the bluff has consequences that last weeks. A body that has not done the long run does not have the tendons, the fueling habits, or the pacing sense to survive a bad day at a long distance. The bib does not give you those. Only the long run does.

So: build it, then buy it. In that order.
