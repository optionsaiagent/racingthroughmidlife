There is a version of starting triathlon in your fifties that begins with a shopping cart. We know because we have watched people do it. The bike is beautiful. The wetsuit is beautiful. The first race is still a first race.

We did it the other way around, mostly by accident, and it turned out to be the right way.

## Honu on the old bike

Jay's first 70.3 was June 3, 2023, in the lava fields between Waikoloa and Hawi. He rode it on the bike he already had. It was not a triathlon bike. It was the bike that had done the long rides, the Haleiwa Metric Century, the brick workouts up the hills. It had earned the race by being there for the training.

He finished. The bike was not the reason he was slow on the climb to Hawi. The wind was, and so was being fifty and eleven months into taking this seriously.

## Then the bike

In July, a month after Honu, the triathlon bike showed up. It is in the second week of July video, along with new shoes and a snorkeling trip to Sharks Cove, because that is what a week looks like in a house where the season is long.

By then the purchase made sense. There was a sprint tri at the end of July, a 112-mile ride in August, and a full-distance decision on the horizon. The bike had a job. It was not a hope.

A month after that, Michelle had a proper bike fit. That is the [other half](/notes/bike-fit-at-fifty) of this lesson: the fit mattered more than the frame, and it should have come first for both of us.

## Why the order matters at fifty

At this age we have the money to buy the thing before we have earned it. That is exactly the trap. The gear makes the decision feel made. Then the first race is hard, the way first races are, and the bike goes to the garage to be a reminder.

Race the first one on what you have. Let the race decide if there is a second one. Then buy the bike, and ride it a lot, alone, in the heat, before it ever sees a transition area.
