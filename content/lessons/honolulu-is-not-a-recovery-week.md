We watch it happen every year. Somebody flies in from a cool place with a great training block behind them, lands Thursday, spends Friday at the beach, and races Sunday in conditions their body has never met.

The finish line photos still look good. The middle of the race didn't.

## The island is a race condition

The Honolulu Triathlon, the Great Aloha Run, the Hapalua, Hibiscus, the Honolulu Marathon. These are real races that real people train hard for. The course isn't gentle because there are palm trees on it. The marathon starts at 5 a.m. specifically so the front of the field gets some running done before the sun goes to work. The back of the field doesn't get that gift.

What gets visitors isn't the heat. Most people have run in 85 degrees. It's the humidity. Sweat doesn't evaporate, so the cooling you're used to just doesn't happen, and your heart rate drifts up at a pace that felt easy at home. You're not out of shape. You're in different physics.

## What we tell friends who are flying in

Get here early. Not a week early, most people can't. But early enough to run twice at the hour the race starts. Your body picks up the temperature faster than you'd think, but it doesn't pick it up from a hotel room.

Drink and salt in the days before, on real food. Not because we sell anything. Because the first hour of a hot race gets decided in the 48 hours before it, and you can't catch up mid-race.

Then run the first third easier than you think you should. Every race here turns into a heat race by the second half. The people who look strong at the end are the ones who were bored at the start.

## The pretty course problem

Diamond Head is a climb. The Kalanianaole out-and-back to Hawaii Kai is exposed for miles. The Honu bike goes to Hawi through lava where the wind has opinions. It's all beautiful. None of it is a recovery week.

Come race here. We'd love to see you. Just bring the respect you'd bring to a hard course anywhere else, and then add the dew point.
