If you watch our 2023 videos in order you'll notice the plan keeps changing. That wasn't a lack of discipline. That was the year.

## February: the rib

Third week of February, Michelle hurt a rib over the weekend. We're not going to diagnose it on a website, because we're not doctors and we don't know your ribs either. What it did to training is simple enough: a rib changes how you breathe, and breathing is most of endurance sport.

The plan for March had a 10K and a half marathon in it. The plan was in pencil. Some of it happened, some of it happened slower, and the [Ku'ikahi 10K](/races/kuikahi-10k-2023) in March ended with Michelle winning her age group anyway, which tells you how much of a race is the last three weeks and how much is the two years before them.

## June: the family

The week after Jay's first 70.3 wasn't a recovery week. It was a week with kids in the house, then nieces and a nephew, then hikes up Diamond Head and out to Makapuu. Running came back in the last week of June, after everybody flew home.

We used to count weeks like that as lost. Now we count them as the reason we do any of this. Nobody's going to remember the tempo run we skipped. Everybody's going to remember the hike.

## Every month: the job

Jay's day job has a calendar that doesn't know what a taper is. Michelle's weeks have their own shape. Some weeks the plan was three sessions and all three happened at 5 a.m. because that was the only hour nobody else wanted.

## What pencil means

It doesn't mean the plan's optional. It means the plan has a pecking order. Every week has one or two sessions that are the reason the week exists: the long run, the long ride, the brick. Those are in pen. Everything else is in pencil, and pencil gets erased when a rib or a nephew or a closing shows up.

Keeping the season alive through the bad weeks mattered more than nailing the good ones. The season's long. The week is short. Protect the season.
