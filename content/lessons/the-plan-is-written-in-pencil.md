If you watch our 2023 videos in order, you will notice that the plan keeps changing. That was not a lack of discipline. That was the year.

## February: the rib

In the third week of February, Michelle hurt a rib over the weekend. We are not going to diagnose it on a website, because we are not doctors and we do not know your ribs either. What we will say is that a rib injury changes everything about how you breathe, and breathing is most of endurance sport.

The plan for March had a 10K and a half marathon in it. The plan was written in pencil. Some of it happened, some of it happened slower, and the [Ku'ikahi 10K](/races/kuikahi-10k-2023) in March ended with Michelle winning her age group anyway, which tells you something about how much of a race is the last three weeks and how much is the two years before them.

## June: the family

The week after Jay's first 70.3 was not a recovery week. It was a week with kids in the house, then nieces and a nephew, then hikes up Diamond Head and out to Makapuu. The running came back in the fourth week of June, after everyone flew home.

We used to treat weeks like that as lost. Now we treat them as the reason we do any of this. Nobody is going to remember the tempo run we skipped. Everybody is going to remember the hike.

## Every month: the job

Jay has a day job with a calendar that does not know what a taper is. Michelle's weeks have their own shape. Some weeks the plan was three sessions and all three happened at 5 a.m. because that was the only hour nobody else wanted.

## What pencil means

It does not mean the plan is optional. It means the plan has a hierarchy. Every week has one or two sessions that are the reason the week exists: the long run, the long ride, the brick. Those are in pen. Everything else is in pencil, and pencil gets erased when a rib or a nephew or a closing shows up.

Keeping the season alive through the bad weeks mattered more than nailing the good ones. That is the whole lesson. The season is long. The week is short. Protect the season.
