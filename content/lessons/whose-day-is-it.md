Most couples-training advice is a lie told with a smile. You will not hold the same wattage. You will not want the same long-run pace. One of you will be ready to talk at mile eight and the other will be conducting a private argument with a hamstring.

In our house the engines do not match. Jay is faster. That is not a secret and it is not the point. The point is what you do about it.

## The rule

Before every shared session, one person's name goes on the workout. That person is the engine. They set the pace, they pick the effort, they say when it is over. The other person is the governor. Their entire job is to sit on that pace and keep it honest.

That is the whole rule. It sounds too simple to matter. It has settled more arguments than anything else we do.

## What it looked like at Hibiscus

May 2023 was Michelle's day. She had a PR in her legs and a half marathon in front of her. Jay's job was not to run his race. It was to take a pace that was easy for him, hold it exactly, and make sure she stayed on it through Diamond Head and the flat, hot miles after.

She ran 2:12. A PR. Jay felt fine afterward, which was the correct outcome, because it was not his day. A week later at Honu it was his day and nobody was pacing him through the lava. That was also correct.

## Where the rule extends

It is not only the long run. It is who gets the early ocean swim and who gets the later ride. It is which weekend gets the brick and which gets the recovery. It is the week when one of us is tapering for a race and the other is in the middle of a build, and the taper wins the calendar because the race is closer.

We do not always get it right. When we get it wrong it is almost always because nobody said whose day it was and both of us assumed.

## The kitchen clause

One more part of the rule that has nothing to do with pace. Dinner is not a workout. If the evening becomes a negotiation about protein, or who ate what, or whether the recovery shake counts, the week has already gone sideways. Eat, talk about something else, go to bed early. Dawn comes fast here.
