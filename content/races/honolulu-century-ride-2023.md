The Honolulu Century is the big organized ride on the island. Late September, out of Kapiolani Park, over the east side and up the windward coast and back, with turnarounds at 25, 50, 75, and 100 miles so nobody has an excuse. We took the 100.

Out through Hawaii Kai, around the southeast tip, and then north with the mountains on your left and the ocean on your right for as long as you care to keep going. It's the same coast the [Kailua 10-Miler](/races/kailua-10-miler-2023) and the [Windward Half](/races/windward-half-2023) use, and it comes with the same wind. One direction's a gift and the other's a tax, and the ride is long enough that you pay it either way.

The Hawaii Bicycling League has run this for decades, and the rest stops are real rest stops with real food, not a folding table with a jug of water on it. A month after the [Dick Evans](/races/dick-evans-112-2023), where 112 miles was a race, a hundred miles with support and a better view felt almost like a day off. Almost.

## The wind

If you take the 100, start early, eat at every stop, and remember the way home is into the trades. The people who go out fast with the wind at their back are usually the people you pass on the return leg, and occasionally they're you.

If you ride on Oahu and haven't done this one, do it. Pick the distance that matches where you are in the season. The 25 and the 50 are real rides with real hills, and nobody at the finish asks which turnaround you took.

## Where it sat

September 2023 had the [Runner's HI 20K](/races/runners-hi-20k-2023) and the [Waikiki swim](/races/waikiki-2-4-mile-swim-2023) earlier in the month. The running series was carrying the marathon. The long rides were carrying the full-distance question. September was the month both of those were loudest, and the following weekend was the [Hybrid Design 25K](/races/hybrid-design-25k-2023), run easy, for reasons that should be obvious to anyone who's just read this page.
