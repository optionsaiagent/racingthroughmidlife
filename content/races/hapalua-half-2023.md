The Hapalua bills itself as Hawaii's half marathon, and it earns it. Big field, Waikiki start, Diamond Head in the middle, and by April the start-line temperature is a preview of what June will feel like.

## The race

Out of Waikiki, along the water, up and around Diamond Head, and back. The Diamond Head climb is the same one the marathon uses, and it does the same thing to your pace at mile eight of a half that it does at mile 24 of a marathon, just with less warning.

## The two weeks around it

The week before had mile repeats in it, the first real speed work of the year. The week after had, in the words of the video title, a brutal brick workout on hills, because the Honolulu Triathlon was five weeks away and Honu was seven, and the bike had to start mattering.

That is what April looked like: a half marathon in the middle of a triathlon build, used as the long run, with the speed work before it and the bike work after it. The half was not the goal. It was the long run that happened to have a medal.

## Lessons learned

The third-week April video is titled "lessons learned from the Hapalua." We are not going to rewrite the video. Watch it. The short version is what every hot half marathon teaches: the first four miles felt easy because it was early, and the last four miles collected on that.

## What we would tell a friend

If you fly in for a Honolulu race, the Hapalua in April is a good one to pick. The field is big enough to feel like an event and the distance is honest. Read [Honolulu is not a recovery week](/lessons/honolulu-is-not-a-recovery-week) first.
