Jay didn't want a marathon. Not in the way people say they don't want one and then sign up anyway. He didn't like running, he'd never run past eight miles, and he'd built a pretty comfortable identity around not being a marathon guy.

Then Michelle stood at the 2021 finish line and said we should do it next year, for his fiftieth. There's no good argument against a birthday.

## The year

The training wasn't dramatic. It was consistent, which at this age is the only kind that works. Dawn runs before the day job. The long run built through the spring until a [half at Hibiscus](/races/hibiscus-half-2022) in May proved 13.1 was possible. Then the long run kept building through a Honolulu summer, which is the part of this story that deserves more sympathy than it gets. Long runs in August here start in the dark and finish in a different climate.

We signed up once the half was done. Not before. The [long run had to exist first](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run).

## The race

The Honolulu Marathon starts at 5 a.m. at Ala Moana, in the dark, with fireworks. Through Waikiki, up and around Diamond Head, out the long flat exposed stretch toward Hawaii Kai, back the same way, and over Diamond Head again to finish at Kapiolani Park. There's no cutoff, which is one of the reasons it's the right first marathon for people who aren't fast. Nobody sweeps you. You finish when you finish.

The second Diamond Head, around mile 24, is where the race tells you the truth about your training. The flat part before it, in the sun, is where it tells you the truth about your eating.

We finished. We're not putting a time on this page we don't have in front of us, and this race was before the log. When the official result's in hand it goes here.

## What it changed

Everything after this exists because of this. Not because the marathon was the goal. Because it proved a long day was survivable at fifty, with the right shoes, the right food, and a plan written in pencil.

Midlife isn't a sports car, for us. It turned out to be this.

A year later we ran it [again](/races/honolulu-marathon-2023), with a camera.
