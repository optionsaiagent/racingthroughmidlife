Jay didn't want a marathon. Not in the way people say they don't want one and then sign up anyway. He didn't like running, he'd never run past eight miles, and he'd built a pretty comfortable identity around not being a marathon guy. (He'd tell you that himself.)

Then I stood at the 2021 finish line and said we should do it next year, for his fiftieth. I don't know what I expected him to say. There's no good argument against a birthday, and he didn't find one.

## The year

The training wasn't dramatic. It was consistent, which at this age is the only kind that works, and honestly it's the only kind I'm any good at. I'm a routine person. Dawn runs before the day job, and the long run built through the spring until a [half at Hibiscus](/races/hibiscus-half-2022) in May proved 13.1 was possible. Then the long run kept building through a Honolulu summer, which deserves more sympathy than it gets. Long runs in August here start in the dark and finish in a different climate.

We signed up once the half was done. Not before. The [long run had to exist first](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run). That was the deal I'd made with myself, because I was the one who'd opened my mouth at that finish line.

## The race

The Honolulu Marathon starts at 5 a.m. at Ala Moana, in the dark, with fireworks. Through Waikiki, up and around Diamond Head, out the long flat exposed stretch toward Hawaii Kai, back the same way, and over Diamond Head again to finish at Kapiolani Park. There's no cutoff, which is one of the reasons it's the right first marathon for people who aren't fast. Nobody sweeps you. You finish when you finish. I needed that, because I was not fast.

The second Diamond Head, around mile 24, is where the race tells you the truth about your training. The flat part before it, in the sun, is where it tells you the truth about your eating.

We finished. Jay in 4:22:02, me in 5:42:20. Those are the official chip times, and they're the first two numbers in the log. Was I slow? Yes. Did I care, standing in Kapiolani Park with a medal on? Not even a little.

## What it changed

Everything on this site exists because of this day. Not because the marathon was the goal. Because it proved a long day was survivable at fifty, with the right shoes, the right food, and a plan written in pencil.

A year later we ran it [again](/races/honolulu-marathon-2023), with a camera.
