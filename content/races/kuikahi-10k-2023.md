I want to be upfront: I didn't go into this race thinking about winning anything. I went in thinking about my rib.

## What was going on

Four weeks before this race I hurt a rib. I'm not going to diagnose it here, and I'm not going to tell you what to do about yours. What it did to my training is simple. Breathing is most of running, and a rib turns breathing into a negotiation. The plan for March had been written in pencil and a lot of it got erased.

I raced anyway, at a distance short enough not to be dumb about it, on a day the rib allowed. Did I think that was smart? I thought it was a 10K. That was about as far as the thinking went.

## The result

First in my age group. This race wasn't timed by Timeline Hawaii, so I don't have the official time in front of me yet, and I'm not going to guess at one. But you don't forget the first time you win something with a number pinned to your shirt. I was confused. Then I wasn't. When the official time turns up it goes right next to this.

## What it says about training

A race isn't the last three weeks. It's the two years before them. I had a marathon in December, a half in January, and a consistent, boring, routine-based year behind that (that's me, I'm the boring one, and it turns out boring wins age groups). A month of messed-up training didn't erase it. That's worth remembering at this age: a bad month is a bad month, not a lost year.
