The Dick Evans is a bike race that goes all the way around Oahu. 112 miles. If that number sounds familiar, it is because it is the bike leg of a full-distance Ironman. That was not a coincidence in our house.

## Why 112

By August 2023 the full distance had gone from a thing we did not talk about to a thing we talked about in the car. Jay had a 70.3 done. The marathon was on the calendar for December. The one leg of a 140.6 that neither of us had done in isolation was the bike, and here was the island offering exactly that distance, with a start line, on a Sunday.

So it was a race, and it was also the longest [bridge](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run) we had built. If 112 miles was survivable alone, in August, it was at least imaginable with a swim before it and a marathon after.

## The ride

Around the island means around the island: town, the east side, the windward coast, the North Shore, and back down the west and center. It is a long day with real traffic, real wind on the windward and north sides, and real August heat by the time you are anywhere near done. The Hawaii Bicycling League runs it in memory of Dick Evans, and it has the feel of an event that has been happening for a very long time.

Jay rode it on the triathlon bike that had arrived six weeks earlier and had done one sprint. That is the shortest runway we would recommend for a new bike before a 112-mile day, and we would recommend a longer one.

## What 112 miles teaches

Fueling, mostly. A 56-mile ride can be done on a good breakfast and a couple of bottles. A 112-mile ride cannot. Everything that was going to be on the list for the full distance had to survive this day first, and some of it did not. The [food that survived a brick](/notes/food-that-survived-a-brick) note is about how that list gets made, and this ride was the biggest audition on it.

The other thing it teaches is that the last thirty miles of a long ride are a different ride. The legs are fine. The neck, the hands, the feet, and the will are the problem. A [bike fit](/notes/bike-fit-at-fifty), which Michelle had the week before this, turns out to be the thing that decides whether mile 90 is uncomfortable or impossible.

## What it changed

After this, the full distance stopped being a hypothetical. Every leg had been done alone. The rest was a matter of putting them in a row on one day, and [funding the season with sleep](/lessons/sleep-funds-the-season). That took another year and a half, which is about right.
