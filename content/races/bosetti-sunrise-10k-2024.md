There's a 10K in Hawaii Kai on the morning of January 1. It starts at sunrise, which on this island is a civilized hour, and it's full of people who decided the year should start with a run instead of a hangover. I'm one of those people. A start line I understand. A party I have to work at.

## A race to attend

Three weeks after the [Honolulu Marathon](/races/honolulu-marathon-2023), my legs were recovered and my routine wasn't. [January](/notes/january-the-five-pounds-and-the-groove) always needs a reason to get the alarm back, and a bib on the first morning of the year is a pretty good reason. It's not a race to race. It's a race to attend. 1:03:26 for me, 50:36 for Jay. Eighth in my age group, fourth in his, and on January 1 that's plenty.

Hawaii Kai is the east end of the marathon course, the flat, exposed stretch that hurt three weeks earlier. On New Year's morning it's a different place: cool, quiet, and over quickly. It's strange to run a road that hurt you and have it be nice to you. I didn't quite trust it.

The people at that start line aren't the fittest people on the island. They're the ones who'll still be running in March, because they never stopped. I wanted to be counted with them, so I set the alarm.

Next on the calendar was the [Kailua 10-Miler](/races/kailua-10-miler-2024), where Jay won his age group for the first time.
