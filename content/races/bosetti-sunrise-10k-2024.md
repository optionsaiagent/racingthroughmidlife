There's a 10K in Hawaii Kai on the morning of January 1. It starts at sunrise, which on this island is a civilized hour, and it's full of people who decided the year should start with a run instead of a hangover.

## A race to attend

Three weeks after the [Honolulu Marathon](/races/honolulu-marathon-2023), the legs were recovered and the routine wasn't. [January](/notes/january-the-five-pounds-and-the-groove) always needs a reason to get the alarm back, and a bib on the first morning of the year is a pretty good reason. It's not a race to race. It's a race to attend.

Hawaii Kai is the east end of the marathon course, the flat, exposed stretch that hurts in December. On New Year's morning it's a different place: cool, quiet, and over quickly.

If you live somewhere with a New Year's Day race, do it, and not for the time. The people at that start line aren't the fittest people on the island. They're the ones who'll still be running in March, because they never stopped.

The next thing on the calendar was a [trail run out to Kaena Point](/races/kaena-point-10-mile-2024), which is about as far from a Hawaii Kai 10K as two runs on one island can get.
