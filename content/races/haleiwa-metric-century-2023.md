A metric century is 100 kilometers, call it 62 miles. In April 2023 that was the longest ride either of us had done in an organized event, and it was five weeks before a 70.3 with a 56-mile bike leg.

## Riding with other people

North Shore, out of Haleiwa. The Hawaii Bicycling League has been putting on rides here for a long time and it shows: marked course, rest stops, people who know what they're doing. All the long rides before this had been solo at dawn, so riding in a group with support felt like a different sport. It's a nicer one.

## Fifty-six miles stopped being theoretical

The Honu bike leg is 56 miles through lava with wind. Before this ride that number was an idea. After it, it was a number we'd already gone past, which is the whole reason a [bridge race](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run) exists.

Sixty-two miles is also long enough to run out of food, to find out where the bike fit's wrong, and to learn that the last fifteen miles of a long ride are a different ride. Better to learn all that in April with a rest stop every twenty miles than in June in the lava. If you're building toward a long-course tri, do one organized ride longer than the race's bike leg. The fitness helps, but the fueling and the hours in the saddle are what you're really there for.

Jay rode this on the bike he had, not a triathlon bike. [The tri bike showed up in July](/lessons/the-bike-came-after-the-race), after the race it would've been bought for was already done.
