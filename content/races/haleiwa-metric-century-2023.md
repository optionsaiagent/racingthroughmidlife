A metric century is 100 kilometers, about 62 miles. In April 2023 that was the longest ride either of us had done in an organized event, and it was five weeks before a 70.3 with a 56-mile bike leg in it.

## The ride

The North Shore, out of Haleiwa. The Hawaii Bicycling League has been running organized rides on this island for a long time, and it shows: marked course, rest stops, people who know what they are doing. For someone whose long rides had all been solo at dawn, riding in a group with support was a different sport.

## Why it mattered

The 70.3 bike leg is 56 miles through lava with wind. Before this ride, the idea of 56 miles was theoretical. After it, it was a number we had exceeded. That is the whole point of a [bridge race](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run): it makes the next distance something you have already been near.

Jay did this on the bike he had, not a triathlon bike. [The tri bike came in July](/lessons/the-bike-came-after-the-race), after the race it would have been bought for was already done.

## What we would tell a friend

If you are building toward a long-course triathlon, do at least one organized ride longer than the race's bike leg. Not for the fitness, though that helps. For the fueling and the hours in the saddle. Sixty-two miles is long enough to run out of food, to find out where your bike fit is wrong, and to learn that the last fifteen miles of a long ride are a different ride. Better to learn all of that in April with a rest stop every twenty miles than in June in the lava.
