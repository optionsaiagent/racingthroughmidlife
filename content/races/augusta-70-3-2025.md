Twenty-nine minutes for 1.2 miles. I'd love to tell you I swam it, but the Savannah River did most of the work. Augusta is famous for its swim, which goes downriver with the current, and if you put my 29:16 next to any of my Honu swims, the difference is the current, not me. Jay's was 30:05. Same river, and I beat him in it by 49 seconds. I beat him out of the water at [Texas](/races/ironman-texas-2025) too, and I'm not going to let go of either one.

Both of us set 70.3 bests here, Jay 5:16:17 and me 6:26:20! Some of that is the river. Some of it is a flat, fast bike in cool late-September air after a summer of training in Honolulu. Heat is the fourth sport for us, and this was the first race in a long time where it didn't show up at all. I didn't know what to do with myself, in a good way.

It was also a rehearsal, as three weeks later was [IRONMAN California](/races/ironman-california-2025). Two long-course races, two trips to the mainland, two bike boxes, in one month. For someone who likes her routine (predictable, boring, and happy that way), that month was a lot. [Race week is a logistics problem](/lessons/race-week-is-a-logistics-problem), and that month was the proof, two bike boxes in thirty days.
