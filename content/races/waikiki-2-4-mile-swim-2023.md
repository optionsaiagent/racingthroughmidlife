A full-distance triathlon starts with a 2.4-mile swim. Before we put that in front of a 112-mile ride and a marathon, we wanted to know what 2.4 miles of ocean felt like on its own.

## The swim

Waikiki, in September, in the open ocean. The water here is warm and usually calm in the morning, but 2.4 miles is a long way to swim in anything, and the ocean has current and chop and a horizon that does not get closer as fast as you want it to.

For a lot of people coming to triathlon after 45, the swim is the thing they are most worried about, and they are right to be. It is the leg with the least margin for a bad day. You can walk a run. You can soft-pedal a bike. You cannot take a break from swimming.

## Why it was there

Same logic as the [112-mile ride](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) two weeks earlier: do each leg of the full distance alone before doing them in a row. The swim was the last one to check. After this, every leg of a 140.6 had been done in isolation.

It was also the week we started paying attention to [zone 2](/notes/zone-2-in-a-sauna), which sounds unrelated but is not. A long ocean swim is an aerobic effort where panic is the enemy, and the discipline of staying easy is the same discipline.

## What we would tell a friend

If you are thinking about long-course triathlon and the swim scares you, do an organized open-water swim at the race distance before you sign up. With kayaks and buoys and other people, not alone. It will either tell you the swim is fine, or it will tell you the swim is the thing to work on for a year. Both answers are worth the entry fee.
