A note on the record first: neither of us shows up in the official Waikiki Roughwater Swim results for 2023. So this was the distance, not the race. We swam it, we didn't get a bib for it, and the page is here because the swim mattered to the season.

A full-distance triathlon opens with a 2.4-mile swim. Before we put that in front of a 112-mile ride and a marathon, we wanted to know what 2.4 miles of ocean felt like on its own.

## The leg you can't walk

Waikiki in September. The water's warm and usually calm in the morning, but 2.4 miles is a long way to swim in anything, and the ocean has current and chop and a horizon that doesn't get closer as fast as you'd like.

If you come to triathlon after 45, the swim is probably the leg you're worried about, and you're right to be. It's the one with the least room for a bad day. You can walk a run. You can soft-pedal a bike. Nobody's ever soft-pedaled the ocean.

## Why it was on the calendar

Same logic as the [112-mile ride](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) two weeks earlier: do each leg of the full distance by itself before doing them in a row. The swim was the last one to check. After this morning, every leg of a 140.6 had been done at least once, just never on the same day.

It was also the week we started paying attention to [zone 2](/notes/zone-2-in-a-sauna), which sounds like a different subject and isn't. A long ocean swim is an aerobic effort where panic is the enemy, and staying easy in the water is the same skill as staying easy anywhere else.

If long-course triathlon is on your mind and the swim scares you, do an organized open-water swim at the race distance before you sign up for anything. With kayaks and buoys and other people around, not by yourself. Either it tells you the swim's fine, or it tells you the swim is what you're working on for the next year. Both answers are worth the entry fee.
