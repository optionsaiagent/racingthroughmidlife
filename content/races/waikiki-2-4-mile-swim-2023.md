A note on the record first: neither of us shows up in the official Waikiki Roughwater Swim results for 2023. So this was the distance, not the race. We swam it, we didn't get a bib for it, and the page is here because the swim mattered to the season. I'm not going to pretend it was more official than it was.

A full-distance triathlon opens with a 2.4-mile swim. Before we put that in front of a 112-mile ride and a marathon, I wanted to know what 2.4 miles of ocean felt like by itself. Not in a pool, not in my head the night before, in the water.

## The leg you can't walk

Waikiki in September. The water's warm and usually calm in the morning, but 2.4 miles is a long way to swim in anything, and the ocean has current and chop and a horizon that doesn't get closer as fast as you'd like.

If you came to triathlon at this age like I did, the swim is probably the leg you're worried about, and I'm not going to tell you you're wrong. It's the one with the least room for a bad day. You can walk a run. You can soft-pedal a bike. Nobody's ever soft-pedaled the ocean. I can have anxiety on dry land about a start line and still get through it. In the water, with the shore going away behind me, it's a different animal.

## Why it was on the calendar

Same logic as the [112-mile ride](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) two weeks earlier: do each leg of the full distance by itself before doing them in a row. The swim was the last one to check. After this morning, every leg of a 140.6 had been done at least once, just never on the same day. Was that reassuring? Sort of. It's like knowing all the ingredients are in the house and not having cooked the meal.

It was also the week we started paying attention to [zone 2](/notes/zone-2-in-a-sauna), which sounds like a different subject and isn't. A long ocean swim is an aerobic effort where panic is the enemy, and staying easy in the water is the same skill as staying easy anywhere else. I'm still working on it. Both of them.

Either a morning like this tells you the swim's fine, or it tells you what you're working on for the next year. Mine said the swim wasn't the thing that was going to stop me. Good enough. It was 2.4 miles, and I was standing on the sand at the end of it.
