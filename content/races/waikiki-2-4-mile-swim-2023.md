I want to be upfront about the numbers on this one. The timing company's results page for the 2023 Roughwater doesn't show either of us when you search it, so the times on the card are ours: Jay in 1 hour 50 minutes, me in 2 hours and 1 minute. If the official sheet ever turns up, it goes on the card and this paragraph goes away.

## Why this swim

A full-distance triathlon opens with a 2.4-mile swim, and before we put that in front of a 112-mile ride and a marathon we wanted to know what 2.4 miles of ocean felt like on its own. The Roughwater is that swim, off Waikiki, in September, in the open ocean with a current and chop and a horizon that doesn't get closer as fast as you'd like.

It's also the swim the very first Ironman used. In February 1978 the original race was this swim, a lap of the island on the bike, and the Honolulu Marathon, done back to back. We didn't know that when we signed up. We found out later that we'd done all three of those legs in 2023, just on three different days: this one, the [Dick Evans](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) in August, and the [marathon](/races/honolulu-marathon-2023) in December.

## The leg you can't walk

If you come to triathlon in your forties, the swim is probably the leg you're worried about, and honestly you're right to be. You can walk a run. You can soft-pedal a bike. Nobody has ever soft-pedaled the ocean. Two hours is a long time to be out there with nothing to hold onto, and I spent a fair amount of it reminding myself that panic doesn't float.

## Why it was on the calendar

Same idea as the [112-mile ride](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) two weeks earlier: do each leg of the full distance by itself before doing them in a row. The swim was the last one to check. After this morning every leg of a 140.6 had been done at least once, just never on the same day. It was also the week we started paying attention to [zone 2](/notes/zone-2-in-a-sauna), which sounds like a different subject and isn't. A long ocean swim is an aerobic effort where panic is the enemy, and staying easy in the water is the same skill as staying easy anywhere else.

If long-course triathlon is on your mind and the swim scares you, an organized open-water swim at the race distance, with kayaks and buoys and other people around, will tell you one of two things. Either the swim's fine, or the swim is what you're working on for the next year. Both answers are worth the entry fee. Mine was the second one, and I'm still working on it.
