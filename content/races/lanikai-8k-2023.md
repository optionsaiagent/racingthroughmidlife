After the 70.3, June was family in the house and hikes and a slow drift back to running. July was about finding some speed again, and an 8K is exactly the right size for that.

Lanikai is a small residential loop on the windward side, the neighborhood with the beach that ends up on postcards. An 8K there is fast and flat and done before the heat fully arrives, which in July means humid instead of punishing. It's worth the drive over the Pali just for the loop.

We ran it because six weeks of long-course training and a race in the lava had left the legs with no memory of what fast felt like. The same week had sprints in it, actual short hard efforts, for the first time since spring. An 8K with a bib was somewhere to put that, and five miles is short enough to race without wrecking the week after.

If you've just done something long, our suggestion is don't go straight back to long. Go short for a few weeks and remind the legs they have gears. The long stuff feels easier when it comes back.

Both of us were second in our age groups, which for an 8K in July is a nice surprise to find on a timing sheet.

The week after mattered, too. The [Na Wahine & Kane sprint triathlon](/races/na-wahine-kane-sprint-2023) was the next weekend, and the new triathlon bike had just shown up.
