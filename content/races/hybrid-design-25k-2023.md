Race three of five. About 15.5 miles, and the first time since the previous December's marathon that either of us had run past the half-marathon distance with a bib on.

The gap between a half and a marathon is where most first-timers get lost. Thirteen miles feels like an accomplishment. Twenty-six feels like a rumor. The 25K and the [30K](/races/boca-hawaii-30k-2023) after it are the two races in the series that close that gap, and they're the reason the series works at all. By November the long run's been past 15 and past 18 with aid stations and a finish line, and December is a step instead of a leap.

This one came a week after the [Honolulu Century](/races/honolulu-century-ride-2023), so a hundred miles on the bike one weekend and 15.5 on foot the next. We ran the second one accordingly: easy, fueled, finished with something left. This is the part of the fall where the [plan is in pencil](/lessons/the-plan-is-written-in-pencil) and the [sleep budget](/lessons/sleep-funds-the-season) decides whether the week works or doesn't.

From here on, every series race was a marathon rehearsal. Same breakfast we'd eat in December, same shoes, same pacing: first third boring, middle third steady, last third honest. Race a 25K in late September and you'll get a great result and a bad October. Nobody needs a great 25K. Next up was the 30K, where that rule gets its real test.
