This is the race that made the marathon real.

## Where we were

In December 2021 Michelle watched the Honolulu Marathon and said we should run it the next year for Jay's fiftieth. At that point the longest run either of us had done was the Great Aloha Run, 8.2 miles. A marathon was not a plan. It was a sentence someone said at a finish line.

The [rule we keep](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run) is that the race does not get paid for until the long run exists. So the first job of 2022 was not the marathon. It was a half. If we could get to 13.1, the marathon became a thing we could picture. If we could not, it moved to the next year, and that would have been fine.

## The race

Hibiscus is on Memorial Day weekend, out of Kapiolani Park, with an early start because by 8 a.m. Honolulu is done being kind. The course takes you around Diamond Head, which is a climb that looks small on a map and feels larger at mile ten in the humidity.

It was the first half marathon for both of us. We are not going to invent how it felt mile by mile, because this was before the camera and before the log, and memory is a liar about pace. We finished. That was the entire goal. The long run now existed.

## What it set up

The marathon got paid for after this. December 2022, the [fiftieth birthday race](/races/honolulu-marathon-for-fifty). Then a year later, at this same race, Michelle ran [2:12](/races/hibiscus-half-2023) and Jay paced her to it. Same course, different people.

If you are 45 and up and thinking about a marathon, this is the rung to stand on first. Not because a half is easy. Because it tells you the truth about the next one before you have paid for it.
