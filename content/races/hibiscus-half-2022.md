I want to be honest about this one: I'm the reason it happened, and I wasn't sure I could do it.

In December 2021 I watched the Honolulu Marathon and told Jay we should run it the next year, for his fiftieth. It came out of my mouth at a finish line, which is the kind of place where things sound easy. The longest either of us had run at that point was the Great Aloha Run, 8.2 miles. A marathon wasn't a plan. It was something I said.

The [rule we keep](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run) is that we don't pay for the race until the long run exists. So the first job of 2022 wasn't the marathon at all. It was a half. If we could get to 13.1, I could picture December. If we couldn't, it would slide to the next year, and I told myself that would be fine. (I'm not sure I believed that. I'd said it out loud already.)

## The race

Hibiscus is Memorial Day weekend out of Kapiolani Park, and it starts early because by 8 a.m. Honolulu is done being nice to you. The course takes you around Diamond Head, a climb that looks small on a map and feels a lot bigger at mile ten in the humidity.

First half marathon for both of us. I'd love to tell you how every mile felt, but this was before the camera and before the log, and my memory of pace is not to be trusted. What I remember is the part that mattered. We finished. That was the whole goal. Now the long run existed.

## What it set up

If you're where I was, thinking about a marathon you haven't earned yet, this is the rung I stood on first. Not because a half is easy. It isn't. Because it tells you the truth about the next one before you've paid for it.

We paid for the marathon after this. December 2022, the [fiftieth birthday race](/races/honolulu-marathon-for-fifty). A year later, same course, I ran [2:12](/races/hibiscus-half-2023) with Jay one second behind me. Same Diamond Head. Different people.
