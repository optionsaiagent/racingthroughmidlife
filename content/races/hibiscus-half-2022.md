I want to be honest about this one: I'm the reason it happened, and I wasn't sure I could do it.

In December 2021 I watched the Honolulu Marathon and told Jay we should run it the next year, for his fiftieth. It came out of my mouth at a finish line, which is the kind of place where things sound easy, and I meant it more than I'd thought it through. The longest either of us had run at that point was the Great Aloha Run, 8.2 miles. A marathon wasn't a plan yet. It was something I'd said out loud, and I'm the kind of person who then has to go and do it.

The [rule we keep](/lessons/dont-sign-up-before-the-long-run) is that we don't pay for the race until the long run exists. So the first job of 2022 wasn't the marathon at all, it was a half. If we could get to 13.1, I could picture December. If we couldn't, it would slide to the next year, and I told myself that would be fine. (I'm not sure I believed that. I'd already said it in front of Jay.)

## The race

Hibiscus is Memorial Day weekend out of Kapiolani Park, and it starts early because by 8 a.m. the heat and humidity in Honolulu are already up and they don't come back down. The course takes you around Diamond Head, a climb that looks small on a map and feels a lot bigger at mile ten when the air is that wet.

First half marathon for both of us. I'd love to tell you how every mile felt, but this was before the camera and before the log, and I can't remember the pace or how the miles went. What I do remember is the part that mattered. We finished! Finishing was the whole goal, and now the long run existed.

## What it set up

This was the rung I stood on first. A half isn't easy, and it wasn't easy for me, but it told me the truth about the marathon before we'd paid for it, and I needed to hear that from my own legs and not from a training plan.

We paid for the marathon after this. December 2022, the [fiftieth birthday race](/races/honolulu-marathon-for-fifty). A year later, same course, I ran [2:12](/races/hibiscus-half-2023) with Jay one second behind me. Same Diamond Head, and I was a different runner by then.
