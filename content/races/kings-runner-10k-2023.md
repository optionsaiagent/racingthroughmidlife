A 10K is the shortest thing we race and the one that tells the most uncomfortable truth. There's nowhere to hide in six miles. The pace you can hold is the pace you've got, and a 10K will tell you that number whether you asked for it or not.

## Why a 10K in March

After a winter of long slow miles and a January that was mostly recovery, I wanted a number. Not a goal-race number, just a reference: what do the legs do when you ask them for faster than marathon pace for forty-some minutes? Whatever the answer was, that's where the spring was going to start from, and the spring had two halves and an Olympic triathlon in it, so it seemed worth knowing. A 10K at real effort also costs a lot less than a half at real effort. It's the cheap way to find out.

This one was a town 10K: flat, early, well run, the whole thing over before the heat gets going. In March on Oahu that means it was merely warm instead of hot. I ran 46:48, 7:33 a mile, eleventh in the 50-54s out of 111, which was the number I was looking for. It wasn't fast enough to change any plans and wasn't slow enough to worry about, which is usually what a reference race tells you.

It also set up the rest of the month. The [Ku'ikahi 10K](/races/kuikahi-10k-2023) came two weeks later, and Michelle won her age group there. The [Windward Half](/races/windward-half-2023) was the week after that.
