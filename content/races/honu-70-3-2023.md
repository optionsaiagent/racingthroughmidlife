Jay spent the week before this race asking himself why he had signed up. That is not false modesty. It is what the week looked like.

## How he got here

Triathlons in 2005 and 2006 that he did not enjoy. A 2014 Honolulu Triathlon and Tin Man with Michelle, without training, that did not take. Then a marathon put on a fiftieth birthday, a half to bridge to it, the marathon itself in December 2022, and a spring of building the bike: the [Haleiwa Metric Century](/races/haleiwa-metric-century-2023) in April, a [50-mile ride and 10-mile run](/notes/fifty-miles-then-ten) in May, and the [Honolulu Triathlon](/races/honolulu-triathlon-2014-and-again) two weeks out as the dress rehearsal.

He did it all on the bike he had. [The triathlon bike came a month after this race](/lessons/the-bike-came-after-the-race), which is the right order and was not entirely planned.

## The course

Honu is the Big Island's 70.3, and it is hard on purpose. The swim is 1.2 miles in the ocean off Waikoloa at dawn, which is the kind part. The bike is 56 miles up the coast to Hawi and back through black lava fields where there is no shade, the wind comes off the water with an opinion, and the road to Hawi climbs roughly 2,400 feet over the course of the day. Then a half marathon, starting around midday, in the heat, on a course that offers almost nothing in the way of cover.

It is the reason [heat is the fourth sport](/notes/heat-is-the-fourth-sport). Everything we believe about humidity, dawn, salt, and pacing the first third easy comes from training for this and from watching what it does to people who did not.

## The day

Michelle was there supporting, and friends were racing, and that mattered more than it should on paper. A first long-course day is a long time to be alone with your doubts. Having people on the course and at the finish is a logistics decision that turns out to be an emotional one.

We are not going to narrate the splits, because we would be guessing, and we do not guess on this site. What we will say is that the swim went the way the Honolulu Tri said it would, the bike was the wind and the climb everyone warned about, and the run was where the race was decided. Jay respected the heat from the first pedal stroke, and the run was survivable because of it. The people walking by mile four were not less fit. They had raced the bike.

He finished. Not to qualify for anything. To find out that the person he had decided he was, the one who did not do this, was out of date.

## What it changed

This was the first race where the distance itself was the point, not the birthday, not the pace, not the other person's day. It made the next question obvious, and the next question was the full distance. That took another year and a half and a [112-mile ride around Oahu](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) to answer.

The official time will go on this page when it is in front of us. Until then: finished, in the lava, at fifty.
