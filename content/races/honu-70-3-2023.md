Jay spent the week before this race asking himself why he'd signed up. That's not false modesty. That's what the week looked like.

## How he got here

Triathlons in 2005 and 2006 that he didn't enjoy. A 2014 Honolulu Triathlon and Tin Man with Michelle, no training, that didn't take. Then a marathon put on a fiftieth birthday, a half to bridge to it, the marathon itself in December 2022, and a spring of building the bike: the [Haleiwa Metric Century](/races/haleiwa-metric-century-2023) in April, a [50-mile ride and 10-mile run](/notes/fifty-miles-then-ten) in May, and the [Honolulu Triathlon](/races/honolulu-triathlon-2014-and-again) two weeks out as the dress rehearsal.

He did all of it on the bike he had. [The tri bike came a month after this race](/lessons/the-bike-came-after-the-race), which is the right order and wasn't entirely on purpose.

## The course

Honu is the Big Island's 70.3, and it's hard on purpose. The swim is 1.2 miles in the ocean off Waikoloa at first light, which is the nice part. The bike is 56 miles up the coast to Hawi and back through black lava where there's no shade, the wind comes off the water with an attitude, and the road to Hawi climbs about 2,400 feet over the course of the day. Then a half marathon, starting around midday, in the heat, on a course that offers you almost nothing in the way of cover.

It's the reason [heat is the fourth sport](/notes/heat-is-the-fourth-sport). Everything we believe about humidity, early alarms, salt, and running the first third easy comes from training for this and from watching what it does to people who didn't.

## The day

Michelle was there supporting, friends were racing, and that mattered more than it looks like it should on paper. A first long-course day is a long time to be alone with your doubts. Having people on the course and at the finish is a logistics decision that turns out to be an emotional one.

The official splits are on the card, and they tell the story better than memory would: 48 minutes in the water, three and a half hours on the bike into that wind, and a 2:16 half marathon in the heat. Jay respected the heat from the first pedal stroke, and the run was survivable because of it. The people walking by mile four weren't less fit. They'd raced the bike.

He finished. Not to qualify for anything. To find out that the guy he'd decided he was, the one who didn't do this, was out of date.

## What it changed

This was the first race where the distance itself was the point. Not the birthday, not the pace, not somebody else's day. It made the next question obvious, and the next question was the full distance. That took another year and a half and a [112-mile ride around Oahu](/races/dick-evans-112-2023) to answer.

6:48:03. 53rd in the age group, and every bit of it earned in the lava, at fifty. He's come back every year since, and the [2024](/races/honu-70-3-2024), [2025](/races/honu-70-3-2025), and [2026](/races/honu-70-3-2026) pages show what a couple more years of this does to a 70.3 time.
