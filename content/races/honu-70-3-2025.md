Was it smart? No. We'd finished [IRONMAN Texas](/races/ironman-texas-2025) on April 26 and Honu was May 31. That's five weeks between a full and a half, and I'll say it plainly: we signed up the week we got home from Texas because the calendar was empty and we didn't know what to do with ourselves, and because Honu is the race we don't skip. I'm not sure anybody asked whether it was a good idea.

6:44:44, tenth in the 45-49s. Four minutes slower than the year before on the clock, but last year's swim was 750 meters and this one was the full 1.2 miles, so on the same course it was really the better day by a long way. The run was 2:25:06, faster than my 2024 run, five weeks after a full Ironman. Jay 5:44:12, eleventh in the 50-54s, and his 1:55 run was faster than his 2024 run too. Jay's word for it is crushed. I'll allow it.

Full swim distance this year, all 1.2 miles of it, unlike the wind-shortened [2024 swim](/races/honu-70-3-2024). 41:53 on the card. I'd waited a year to find out what that swim actually felt like, and now I know, and I'd still take the full one.
