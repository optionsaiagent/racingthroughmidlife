We finished [IRONMAN Texas](/races/ironman-texas-2025) on April 26 and Honu was May 31, which is five weeks between a full and a half. Was that smart? It wasn't, and I'll say it plainly: we signed up the week we got home from Texas because the calendar was empty and we didn't know what to do with ourselves, and because Honu is the race we don't skip, and I'm not sure anybody asked whether it was a good idea.

I came in at 6:44:44, tenth in the 45-49s, which was four minutes slower than the year before on the clock. Last year's swim was 750 meters, though, and this one was the full 1.2 miles, so on the same course it was really the better day by a long way. The run was 2:25:06, faster than my 2024 run, and that was five weeks after a full Ironman. Jay went 5:44:12, eleventh in the 50-54s, and his 1:55 run was faster than his 2024 run too. Jay's word for the day is crushed, and since both of our runs were faster than the year before, I'll allow it.

The swim was the full distance this year, all 1.2 miles of it, unlike the wind-shortened [2024 swim](/races/honu-70-3-2024), and the card has me at 41:53. I'd waited a year to find out what that swim actually felt like, and now that I know, I'd still take the full one.
