Six weeks after a 70.3, a sprint triathlon is a strange thing to do. It is also the smartest race Jay did that summer.

## Why a sprint

Two reasons. The [triathlon bike had arrived in the second week of July](/lessons/the-bike-came-after-the-race), and a bike that has never seen a transition area should see a small one first. And after the long, slow, hot build to Honu, a short hard race is a reset: fast swim, fast bike, fast run, home before the heat does anything serious.

A sprint is where you learn transitions. At a 70.3, a bad transition costs you a few minutes in a day that is hours long, so nobody practices them. At a sprint, a bad transition is a meaningful chunk of the race, so you learn to lay out the towel, rack the bike the right way, and get the shoes on without sitting down. Those lessons carry up to every distance.

## The race

A local sprint on Oahu with a swim, a short bike, and a short run. We are not going to describe the course in detail, because we would be reconstructing it from a video, and the video does that better. Watch it.

## What we would tell a friend

Your first triathlon should be a sprint. Not because you cannot do an Olympic, but because a sprint teaches you everything about the sport that is not fitness: the swim start, the transition, the first mile of running off the bike. Learn those at a race where a mistake costs minutes, not a trip. Then go longer.

And if you buy a new bike mid-season, race it at a sprint before it goes anywhere that matters. Four weeks after this, the same bike did [112 miles around the island](/races/dick-evans-112-2023).
