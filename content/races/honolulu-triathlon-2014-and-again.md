We did this race in 2014. Then we did it in 2023. The course didn't change. We did.

## 2014

Jay talked Michelle into it. Neither of us was really training. We hadn't decided this was a life, just a weekend with a timing chip. We did the Tin Man two months later and then more or less stopped. It didn't take.

We're not going to pretend 2014 was a disaster or a triumph. It was a Sunday. That's the honest description. Without training, an Olympic-distance tri is a long, uncomfortable Sunday, and then you go home.

## 2023

Nine years later, with a marathon and two halves in the previous twelve months and a 70.3 two weeks out, we came back to Ala Moana and raced it. Actually raced it. Sleep managed, bricks done, eating tested on a [50-mile ride and a 10-mile run](/notes/fifty-miles-then-ten) three weeks earlier.

The Olympic distance is where you find out whether you actually like triathlon or you just like the idea of a Saturday with a timing chip. In 2014 we liked the idea. In 2023 we liked the sport, which is a different thing and the reason this website exists.

## The course

Ala Moana Beach Park, May. A swim inside the reef in water that's calm most mornings, a bike out along the highway and back, and a 10K in the park in the heat that's already showed up by the time you get to it. It's the only Olympic-distance tri on the island, so it anchors everybody's spring.

It was also the bridge to Honu. Jay went into the 70.3 knowing his transitions worked and his open-water swim wouldn't panic, because he'd done both here two weeks earlier.

## The comparison that matters

The 2014 Honolulu Tri results aren't online anywhere we could find, but the [Tin Man from that same summer](/races/tinman-triathlon-2014) is, and it's on its own page. The 2023 numbers are on the card: Jay 2:46:58, Michelle 3:14:51, sixth in her age group. The comparison that matters is the weeks before. In 2014 there were none. In 2023 there were fifty. A year later, with a full season of triathlon behind us, the [2024 edition](/races/honolulu-triathlon-2024) went 2:20 and 2:49.

One week later the [Hibiscus Half](/races/hibiscus-half-2023) was Michelle's day. Both races are in the same video.
