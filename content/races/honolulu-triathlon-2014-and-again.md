We did this race in 2014. Then we did it in 2023. The course did not change. We did.

## 2014

Jay talked Michelle into it. Neither of us was really training. We had not decided this was a life, just a weekend with a timing chip. We did the Tin Man two months later and then more or less stopped. The bug had not landed.

We are not going to pretend that 2014 was a disaster or a triumph. It was a Sunday. That is the honest description. Without training, an Olympic-distance triathlon is a long, uncomfortable Sunday, and then you go home.

## 2023

Nine years later, with a marathon and two half marathons in the previous twelve months and a 70.3 two weeks away, we came back to Ala Moana and raced it. Actually raced it: sleep managed, bricks done, fueling tested on a [50-mile ride and a 10-mile run](/notes/fifty-miles-then-ten) three weeks earlier.

The Olympic distance is where you find out whether you actually like triathlon or you just like the idea of a Saturday with a timing chip. In 2014 we liked the idea. In 2023 we liked the sport, which is a different thing and the reason there is a website.

## The race

Ala Moana Beach Park, May. A swim inside the reef in water that is calm most mornings, a bike course out along the highway and back, and a 10K run in the park in the heat that has already arrived by the time you get to it. It is the only Olympic-distance triathlon on the island, so it anchors the spring for everyone here.

It was also the bridge to Honu. Jay went into the 70.3 knowing his transitions worked and his open-water swim would not panic, because he had done both here two weeks earlier.

## The useful comparison

Not the time. We do not have the 2014 result in front of us and we are not going to guess at it. The useful comparison is the weeks before. In 2014 there were none. In 2023 there were fifty.
