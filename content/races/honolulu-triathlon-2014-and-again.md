We did this race in 2014, and then we did it again in 2023. The course didn't change much in nine years. We did.

## 2014

I talked Michelle into it. Neither of us was really training, and we hadn't decided this was going to be a life, just a weekend with a timing chip. We did the [Tin Man](/races/tinman-triathlon-2014) two months later and then more or less stopped. It didn't take.

I'm not going to pretend 2014 was a disaster or a triumph. It was a Sunday. Without any training, an Olympic-distance triathlon is a long, uncomfortable morning, and then you go home and eat, and that's the honest description of it.

## 2023

Nine years later we came back to Ala Moana with a marathon and two halves in the previous twelve months and a 70.3 two weeks out, and this time we actually raced it. The lead-up looked like what a lead-up is supposed to look like: sleep managed, bricks done, eating tested on a [50-mile ride and a 10-mile run](/notes/fifty-miles-then-ten) three weeks earlier. In 2014 the lead-up was whatever we happened to be doing that week, which was usually nothing.

The Olympic distance is where you find out whether you like triathlon or you just like the idea of a Saturday with a timing chip. In 2014 we liked the idea. In 2023 we liked the sport, which is a different thing, and it's the reason this website exists.

## The course

Ala Moana Beach Park in May. The swim is inside the reef, in water that's calm most mornings, and the bike goes out along the highway and back. Then a 10K in the park, in heat that has usually already shown up by the time you get to it. It's the only Olympic-distance tri on the island, so it anchors everybody's spring, ours included.

For me, it was also the bridge to Honu. I went into the 70.3 knowing my transitions worked and that my open-water swim wouldn't panic, because I'd done both here two weeks earlier, and two weeks is about the right amount of time to still remember what went wrong.

## The comparison that matters

The 2014 results aren't online anywhere we could find, but the Tin Man from that same summer is, and it has its own page. The 2023 numbers are on the card: 2:46:58 for me, 3:14:51 for Michelle, sixth in her age group. The comparison that matters isn't the times, though. It's the weeks before. In 2014 there were none. In 2023 there were fifty.

A year later, with a full season of triathlon behind us, the [2024 edition](/races/honolulu-triathlon-2024) went 2:20 and 2:49. And one week after this one, the [Hibiscus Half](/races/hibiscus-half-2023) was Michelle's day. Both races are in the same video.
