About 18.6 miles, seven weeks before the marathon. Of the five races in the series, this is the one that matters most and the one that's easiest to get wrong.

## The trap

A 30K is long enough to feel like an accomplishment and short enough to race. Run it at real effort and it's a marathon in disguise, with a marathon's worth of recovery attached, and there isn't a marathon's worth of recovery available because there's a marathon in seven weeks.

So we ran it at marathon effort. The [rule for the series](/lessons/let-the-calendar-be-the-plan) is that from the 25K on, every race is a rehearsal. The 30K is the one in full costume.

## What we were checking

Breakfast: the one we'd eat in December. Shoes: the pair that would get the marathon bib. Fueling: what was in the pockets and whether it stayed down after mile twelve in the heat. Pace: could the first ten miles be boring on purpose, and what did the last five feel like once they had been.

That's what a 30K is for. A time isn't.

## What we'd tell a friend

If you take one thing from the whole readiness series, take this one. Race the 30K and you'll have a great October and a bad December. Rehearse it and December is a longer version of a day you've already done. The people who look strong at mile 22 of the Honolulu Marathon are the people who were bored at mile 5 of the BOCA 30K.
