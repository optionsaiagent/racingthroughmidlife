About 18.6 miles, seven weeks before the marathon. This is the race in the series that matters most and the one that is easiest to get wrong.

## The trap

A 30K is long enough to feel like an accomplishment and short enough to race. That is the trap. Run at real effort, it is a marathon in disguise, and it takes a marathon's worth of recovery, and you do not have a marathon's worth of recovery available because there is a marathon in seven weeks.

So we ran it at marathon effort. Not 30K effort. The [rule for the series](/lessons/let-the-calendar-be-the-plan) is that from the 25K on, every race is a rehearsal, and the 30K is the full dress.

## What we were checking

Breakfast: the one we would eat in December. Shoes: the ones that would get the marathon bib. Fueling: what was in the pockets and whether it stayed down after mile twelve in the heat. Pace: could the first ten miles be boring on purpose, and what did the last five feel like after that.

The answers to those questions are what a 30K is for. A time is not.

## What we would tell a friend

If you only take one thing from the whole readiness series, take this: the 30K is a rehearsal. Race it and you will have a great October and a bad December. Rehearse it and December is just a longer version of a day you have already done. The people who look strong at mile 22 of the Honolulu Marathon are the people who were bored at mile 5 of the BOCA 30K.
