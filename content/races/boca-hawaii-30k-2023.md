About 18.6 miles, seven weeks before the marathon. Of the five races in the series, this is the one that matters most and the one that's easiest to get wrong, and I understand exactly why people get it wrong.

## The trap

A 30K is long enough to feel like an accomplishment and short enough to race. Run it at real effort and it's a marathon in disguise, with a marathon's worth of recovery attached, and there isn't a marathon's worth of recovery available because there's a marathon in seven weeks. Do the math. I did, more than once, because the number on the card is right there tempting you.

So we ran it at marathon effort. The [rule for the series](/lessons/let-the-calendar-be-the-plan) is that from the 25K on, every race is a rehearsal. The 30K is the one in full costume.

## What I was checking

Breakfast: the one I'd eat in December. Shoes: the pair that would get the marathon bib. Fueling: what was in my pockets and whether it stayed down after mile twelve in the heat. Pace: could the first ten miles be boring on purpose, and what did the last five feel like once they had been. Boring on purpose is my specialty. I've been practicing it my whole life.

That's what a 30K is for. A time isn't. Mine was 3:27:55, 11:09 a mile, and I'm putting it here because it's on the card, not because it means anything.

## Rehearse it

If I took one thing from the whole readiness series, it was this race. Race the 30K and you get a great October and a bad December. Rehearse it and December is a longer version of a day you've already done. The people who look strong at mile 22 of the Honolulu Marathon are the people who were bored at mile 5 of the BOCA 30K. I wanted to be one of those people. Seven weeks later I'd find out.
