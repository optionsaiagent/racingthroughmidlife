A year after [Jay's first](/races/honu-70-3-2023), we went back, and this time it was both of us.

I want to be honest about that decision. I'd been there in 2023, on the side of it, watching that road to Hawi take Jay apart, and I signed up for the same race anyway. Was that smart? I still don't know. It was the one I wanted, and once I'd decided that, the smart part didn't get much of a vote.

6:40:28 and 19th in the 40-44s, my first 70.3! On the course that had spent the previous June humbling Jay, which I'd watched happen the year before. Jay went 5:26:37, which is an hour and twenty-one minutes faster than his 2023 on the same road. Same island, same lava, same wind, and a very different year for him.

About the swim. The card says 16 minutes for both of us, and 16 minutes isn't a 1.2-mile swim. The wind that morning was strong enough to drag the marker buoys around, so the race cut the swim to 750 meters. I'd trained for 1.2 miles and I got 750, and I'm not going to pretend I was upset about it. The wind didn't go anywhere though. It was waiting on the road to Hawi, and the bike and the run were the full distance and the full heat. 3:44:25 on the bike and 2:26:45 on the run for me, and I earned every minute of both, as much as I'd have liked a few of them back.

Two trips to the Big Island for this race now, and it was starting to feel like the thing the year gets built around. It was. We came back in [2025](/races/honu-70-3-2025) and [2026](/races/honu-70-3-2026).
