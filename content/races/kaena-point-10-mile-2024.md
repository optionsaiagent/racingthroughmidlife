Kaena Point is the far western tip of Oahu, where the paved road quits and a dirt track keeps going out to a nature reserve at the point. No shade and no pavement, ocean on one side and dry mountainside on the other, and a trail that's mostly rocks with sand in between.

Ten miles out and back on that. It isn't technical the way a mountain trail is technical, but it's uneven and it's exposed, and the sun comes off the water in a way that makes ten miles feel like more than ten miles. The way back sucks. I'll just say that, and I'll also say I knew it would and went anyway.

Why would two people a month after a 70.3 go run ten miles on rocks with no shade? Because after a year of road racing that ended in a marathon, the legs needed something that wasn't pavement, and this was as far from pavement as the island gets. Different surface, different muscles, and no pace to chase, because the surface makes pace meaningless. I liked that part more than I expected to. There's nothing to compare yourself to out there, which for someone who compares herself to everything was kind of a relief. It's strength work dressed up as a morning out.

It's also stunning. The point is where the island just stops, seabirds everywhere and, in winter, whales offshore. A race that turns around there is worth doing once for the turnaround alone.

A few things I didn't expect. Ten miles doesn't sound like a distance you need to think about water for, and out there it is. My shoes did not come home clean. And the sun was a problem on the way back, so I was glad we hadn't been brave in the first five miles.

We ran it together and finished one second apart, 1:58:36 and 1:58:37, a month after [Honu](/races/honu-70-3-2024), in a year that was mostly about the [decision to go the full distance](/races/going-the-full-distance) and the training that came with it. It's a HURT Hawaii event, held around the Fourth of July, which is why it's called the Firecracker.
