Kaena Point is the far western tip of Oahu, where the paved road quits and a dirt track keeps going out to a nature reserve at the point. No shade. No pavement. Ocean on one side, dry mountainside on the other, and a trail that's mostly rocks with sand in between.

Ten miles out and back on that. It isn't technical the way a mountain trail is technical, but it's uneven, it's exposed, and the sun comes off the water in a way that makes ten miles feel like more than ten miles. The way back sucks. I'll just say that.

Why would two people a month out from a 70.3 go run ten miles on rocks with no shade? Because after a year of road racing that ended in a marathon, the legs needed something that wasn't pavement. Different surface, different muscles, and no pace to chase, because the surface makes pace meaningless. That part I liked more than I expected to. There's nothing to compare yourself to out there. It's strength work dressed up as a morning out.

It's also stunning. The point is where the island just stops, seabirds everywhere and, in winter, whales offshore. A race that turns around there is worth doing once for the turnaround alone.

A few things I'd want to know going in. Ten miles doesn't sound like a distance you need to think about water for; out there, it is. Your shoes will not come home clean. And the sun is going to be a problem on the way back, so the first five miles are not the place to be brave.

We ran it together and finished one second apart, 1:58:36 and 1:58:37, a month after [Honu](/races/honu-70-3-2024), in a year that was mostly about the [decision to go the full distance](/races/going-the-full-distance) and the training that came with it. It's a HURT Hawaii event, held around the Fourth of July, which is why it's called the Firecracker.
