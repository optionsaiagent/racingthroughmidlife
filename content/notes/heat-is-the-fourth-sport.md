We live here. We still get it wrong. That should tell you something about how visitors do.

## Why humidity is the whole story

Heat by itself is a number on a forecast. Humidity decides whether your body can do anything about it. Sweat cools you by evaporating, and when the air's already full of water it doesn't evaporate, it just runs. You're losing fluid and getting none of the cooling for it.

What that means in practice is an effort that felt easy in dry air feels hard here at the same pace, and your heart rate climbs through a long session even when nothing else changes. That drift isn't a fitness problem. It's physics. Pretending otherwise is how people cook themselves at mile nine of a half that started at 5:30 in the morning.

The number to look at is the dew point, not the temperature. When the dew point's in the 70s, which is most of the summer here, every session is a heat session.

## Dawn is a temperature, not a mood

Every long workout we do starts in the dark. Not because we're disciplined. Because it's the only hour the work can happen. By 8 a.m. the pavement's radiating, by 10 the shade's gone, and a long run that starts at 7 finishes in a completely different race than the one it started in.

The local races know this. The Honolulu Marathon starts at 5 a.m. Hibiscus starts early. Honu in June starts with a swim at first light and then sends you into lava fields for a bike ride where shade is a rumor.

So the alarm's early. Everything else follows from that.

## Water and salt, not a product

We don't sell electrolytes and we're not going to recommend a brand. What we'll tell you is what we do: drink before the session, carry more water than seems reasonable, and put salt on real food in the days around a long effort. In this humidity the sweat rate's high and it's salty, and a long day without replacing both ends badly.

On a hot long ride we stop for water more than the plan says. On a long run we carry it. On race day we hit every aid station from the first one, whether we feel like it or not, because by the time you feel like you need it you're already behind.

## What a mainland plan gets wrong here

Any plan written somewhere cool assumes your body can cool itself. Here it can't, so paces are slower for the same effort (that's correct, not a failure), long sessions have to start earlier and end sooner than the plan thinks, recovery takes longer because the body worked harder than the pace suggests, and every race is a heat race, including the "winter" ones.

We don't adjust a plan with a formula. We adjust it with the alarm clock and the dew point.

## Honu, if you want proof

Jay's first 70.3 in June 2023 is this whole note in one race. Swim at first light in the ocean, then 56 miles through black lava with the wind coming off the water and not a tree for miles, then a half marathon in the heat of the day. The people who finished well were the ones who respected the heat from the first pedal stroke. The ones who didn't were walking by mile four of the run. Heat's the fourth sport. Train it like one.
