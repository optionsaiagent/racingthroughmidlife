We live here. We still get it wrong. That should tell you something about how visitors do.

## Why humidity is the whole story

Heat by itself is a number on a forecast. Humidity is what decides whether your body can do anything about it. Sweat cools you by evaporating. When the air is already full of water, it does not evaporate, it just runs. You are losing fluid and getting none of the cooling for it.

The practical result is that an effort that felt easy in dry air feels hard here at the same pace, and your heart rate climbs through a long session even when nothing else changes. That drift is not a fitness problem. It is physics. Pretending otherwise is how people cook themselves at mile nine of a half marathon that started at 5:30 a.m.

The number to look at is the dew point, not the temperature. When the dew point is in the 70s, which it is for most of the summer here, every session is a heat session.

## Dawn is a temperature, not an aesthetic

Every long workout we do starts in the dark. Not because we are disciplined. Because the only hour when the work can happen is the one before the sun. By 8 a.m. the pavement is radiating, by 10 the shade is gone, and a long run that starts at 7 finishes in a completely different race than the one it started in.

Local races know this. The Honolulu Marathon starts at 5 a.m. The Hibiscus Half starts early. Honu in June starts with a swim at dawn and then sends you into lava fields for a bike ride where the shade is a rumor.

So the alarm is early. Everything else follows from that.

## Water and salt, not a product

We do not sell electrolytes and we are not going to recommend one. What we will say is what we do: drink before the session, carry more water than seems reasonable, and put salt on real food in the days around a long effort. In this humidity the sweat rate is high and it is salty, and a long day without replacing both ends badly.

On a hot long ride we stop for water more often than the plan says. On a long run we carry it. On race day we take every aid station from the first one, even when we do not feel like we need it, because by the time you feel like you need it you are already behind.

## What a mainland plan gets wrong here

Any plan written somewhere cool assumes your body can cool itself. Here it cannot, so:

- Paces are slower for the same effort, and that is correct, not a failure.
- Long sessions need to start earlier than the plan thinks, and end before the plan thinks.
- Recovery takes longer because the body worked harder than the pace suggests.
- Every race is a heat race, including the ones in "winter."

We do not adjust a plan by a formula. We adjust it by the alarm clock and the dew point.

## Honu, the proof

Jay's first 70.3 in June 2023 is the whole note in one race. Swim at dawn in the ocean, then 56 miles through black lava with the wind coming off the ocean and no tree for miles, then a half marathon in the heat of the day. The people who finished well were the ones who respected the heat from the first pedal stroke. The ones who did not were walking by mile four of the run. Read [the race report](/races/honu-70-3-2023).

Heat is the fourth sport. Train it like one.
