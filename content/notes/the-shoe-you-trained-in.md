This isn't a shoe review. We're not going to talk about stack height or carbon plates, because we don't race in anything we haven't trained in and we don't name anything we haven't raced in.

Here's what Jay wore and why.

## May 2023

Two pairs in the rotation that month. Asics Gel Nimbus 24 for the training weeks. New Balance Fresh Foam X 1080 v12 for the two races, Hibiscus Half at the end of May and Honu the week after.

The 1080s got the races because they'd already done the long runs. Not because of a spec sheet. Because of a Saturday morning in April when they went past ten miles without a calf filing a complaint.

## The rule

Put the race shoe on a long run before the race. At least one. The longest one in the block if you can.

A new shoe has a drop, a stack, a fit, and an opinion about your foot, and your calf hasn't met any of those yet. Race day isn't the introduction. The long run is. If the long run goes fine, the shoe gets the bib. If it doesn't, you found out on a Saturday with nothing on the line.

## Why we bother writing this down

Because the internet's full of people buying a race shoe the week of the race, and at fifty a calf that disagrees with a shoe at mile ten isn't a nuisance. It's the next three weeks of the season.

Two pairs. One trained, one raced. Nothing new in the box on race morning.
