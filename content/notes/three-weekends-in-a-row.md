We didn't plan it this way. The calendar did. The Honolulu Triathlon's in May. Hibiscus is Memorial Day weekend. Honu's the first weekend of June. In 2023 that lined up as three race weekends in a row, and only the last one was the goal.

## The order mattered

Weekend one, the Olympic tri, was a hard effort for both of us and the race we'd done badly in 2014. It was a test. Weekend two was Michelle's day at Hibiscus, with Jay pacing and very deliberately not racing. Weekend three was Jay's first 70.3, with nobody pacing anybody.

So the load wasn't three hard races each. It was one hard race for both, one hard race for Michelle and an easy long run for Jay, and one hard race for Jay with Michelle supporting. [Whose day is it](/lessons/whose-day-is-it) is what made three weekends survivable. Without it, two people racing three times would've been six hard efforts in fifteen days, and somebody would've broken.

## The week in between

The second week of May, the one in the video, was the quiet one. Short sessions. Sleep. Nothing that'd leave a mark on the weekend. It's boring to watch and it was boring to do, and it was the most important week of the three.

This is the thing we'd tell a friend: the week between races isn't a chance to sneak in the workout you missed. It's a chance to show up at the next start line rested. You can't get fitter in seven days. You can absolutely get more tired.

## What we'd do differently

Not much, given the calendar. Maybe not do the Olympic three weeks out from a 70.3 in some other year. But in 2023 the Olympic was the bridge, and it was worth it. Jay went into Honu knowing his transitions worked and his swim wouldn't panic, because he'd done both at Ala Moana two weeks earlier.

Three weekends in a row isn't a plan. It's a calendar accident you survive with sleep and one honest conversation about whose day each one is.
