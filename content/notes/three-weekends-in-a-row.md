We did not plan it this way. The calendar did. The Honolulu Triathlon is in May. The Hibiscus Half is Memorial Day weekend. Honu is the first weekend of June. In 2023 that lined up as three race weekends in a row, and only one of them, the last one, was the goal.

## The order mattered

Weekend one, the Olympic-distance tri, was a hard effort for both of us and the race we had done badly in 2014. It was a test. Weekend two was Michelle's day at Hibiscus, with Jay pacing and deliberately not racing. Weekend three was Jay's first 70.3, with nobody pacing anybody.

So the load was not three hard races each. It was one hard race, one hard race for Michelle and an easy long run for Jay, and one hard race for Jay with Michelle supporting. The [whose day is it](/lessons/whose-day-is-it) rule is what made three weekends survivable. Without it, two people racing three times would have been six hard efforts in fifteen days, and somebody would have broken.

## The week in between

The second week of May, the one in the video, was the quiet one. Short sessions. Sleep. Nothing that would leave a mark on the weekend. It is boring to watch and it was boring to do, and it was the most important week of the three.

This is the thing we would tell a friend: the week between races is not a chance to sneak in the workout you missed. It is a chance to arrive at the next start line rested. You cannot get fitter in seven days. You can absolutely get more tired.

## What we would do differently

Not much, given the calendar. Maybe not do the Olympic tri three weeks out from a 70.3 in a different year. But in 2023 the Olympic was the bridge, and the bridge was worth it. Jay went into Honu knowing his transitions worked and his swim would not panic, because he had done it in Ala Moana three weeks earlier.

Three weekends in a row is not a plan. It is a calendar accident you survive with sleep and one honest conversation about whose day each one is.
