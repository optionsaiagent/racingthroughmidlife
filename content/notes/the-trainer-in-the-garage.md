It's a strange thing to explain to somebody on the mainland: we live in Honolulu and we ride a bike in the garage staring at a screen. The weather outside is the weather people fly here for.

Here's why it won anyway.

## The problems it solved

**The dark.** The riding window is dawn. Dawn is dark. Riding in traffic in the dark on an island where the roads weren't built with a shoulder in mind is a risk we take on the weekend, when there's time to pick the route. Not on a Tuesday at 5 a.m.

**The clock.** A weekday ride outside is the ride plus getting to a road worth riding plus getting home. On the trainer, an hour is an hour. At fifty with a day job, the hour that's actually an hour is the one that happens.

**The heat.** The trainer's got a fan. The fan doesn't have a dew point. A hard interval session in the garage is a hard interval session, not a hard interval session plus a heat problem. The [zone 2 note](/notes/zone-2-in-a-sauna) gets into why that matters.

**The honesty.** The trainer doesn't have a downhill. Every minute is a minute of pedaling. It's a harder hour than the same hour outside, and it's a measured one.

## How it went

Late October 2023 was the first week: a few rides, a race or two on the app, and the surprise that a virtual race hurts exactly as much as a real one. By November we were hooked. Zwift Academy in November. The Fondo series in December. It became the weekday bike, full stop.

Same week as the first trainer rides, Jay rode over the Pali Highway for the first time, outside, on a real road with a real climb. That's the balance. Trainer for the weekdays and the specific efforts, island for the weekends and the long ones. The trainer didn't replace the road. It replaced the weekday rides that weren't happening.

## If your weekday rides aren't happening

Because of dark, traffic, heat, or time, a trainer fixes all four. It doesn't fix motivation and it won't make you enjoy staring at a wall, but the app helps more than we expected and the hour's a real hour.

It's not cheating. It's the only way we found to ride four days a week and still have jobs.
