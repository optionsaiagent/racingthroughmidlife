A brick is a bike ride followed straight away by a run. The name is what your legs feel like for the first mile of the run. First week of May 2023, a month out from Jay's first 70.3, we did the biggest one of the block: 50 miles on the bike, then 10 on foot.

## Why those numbers

A 70.3 is 56 miles of bike and 13.1 of run. A 50-and-10 gets you close enough to feel the shape of the day without it being the day. It's the bridge. If the run off the bike fell apart at mile three, that was something we needed to know in May, not in June in the lava.

We also did it at the hour the race would happen. Bike at dawn, run into the morning heat. The heat was the point. Honu's run starts around midday and there's no shade on it.

## What we learned

The first mile of the run is a lie. It always feels wrong. Legs that have been pedaling for three hours don't want to run and they let you know it. Miles two and three decide whether it's a bad day or just a brick. That week it was just a brick.

The other thing it taught us was eating. Fifty miles is long enough to run out of whatever you had for breakfast, and if you don't eat on the bike, the run pays for it. The food that came off that ride still in the pockets was the food that hadn't worked. The food that was gone got the bib.

## New shoes, not race shoes

Same week had new shoes in it. They didn't get to race. They got to train, because the [shoe that gets the bib](/notes/the-shoe-you-trained-in) is the one that's already done a long run, and these hadn't.

## The stretch after

This brick was the big one. The two weeks after it had the Honolulu Triathlon and the Hibiscus Half in them, then Honu. That's three race weekends in a row, and the [next note](/notes/three-weekends-in-a-row) is about getting two bodies through that.
