A brick is a bike ride followed immediately by a run. The name is what your legs feel like for the first mile of the run. In the first week of May 2023, a month out from Jay's first 70.3, we did the biggest one of the block: 50 miles on the bike, then 10 miles on foot.

## Why that distance

A 70.3 is 56 miles of bike and 13.1 of run. A 50-and-10 is close enough to feel the shape of the day without being the day. It is the bridge. If the run off the bike fell apart at mile three, that was information we needed in May, not in June in the lava.

We also did it at the hour the race would happen: bike at dawn, run into the morning heat. The heat was the point. Honu's run starts around midday and there is no shade on it.

## What it taught us

The first mile of the run is a lie. It always feels wrong. Legs that have been pedaling for three hours do not want to run and they let you know. The second and third miles decide whether it is a bad day or just a brick. That week, it was just a brick.

The other thing it taught us was fueling. Fifty miles is long enough to run out of what you ate at breakfast, and if you do not eat on the bike, the run pays for it. The food that came off that ride still in the pockets was the food that had not worked. The food that was gone was the food that got the bib.

## New shoes, not race shoes

The same week had new shoes in it. They did not get to race. They got to train, because the [shoe that gets the bib](/notes/the-shoe-you-trained-in) is the one that has already done a long run, and these had not.

## The week after

This brick was the big one. The two weeks after it had the Honolulu Triathlon and the Hibiscus Half in them, then Honu. That is three race weekends in a row, and the [next note](/notes/three-weekends-in-a-row) is about surviving that.
