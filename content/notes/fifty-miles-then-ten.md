A brick is a bike ride followed straight away by a run, and the name is what your legs feel like for the first mile of the run. In the first week of May 2023, a month out from my first 70.3, I did the biggest one of the block: 50 miles on the bike, then 10 on foot.

The numbers weren't random. A 70.3 is 56 miles of bike and 13.1 of run, and a 50-and-10 gets you close enough to feel the shape of the day without it being the day. If the run off the bike was going to fall apart at mile three, that was something I needed to know in May and not in June in the lava. I also did it at the hour the race would happen, bike at dawn and run into the morning heat, because Honu's run starts around midday with no shade on it and the heat was the part I most needed to practice.

## What we learned

The first mile of the run is a lie, it always feels wrong. Legs that have been pedaling for three hours don't want to run, and it takes a couple of miles before you can tell anything from them. Miles two and three usually decide whether it's a bad day or just a brick, and that week it was just a brick.

The other thing it taught us was eating. Fifty miles is long enough to run out of whatever you had for breakfast, and if you don't eat on the bike the run pays for it. Everything that came off that ride still in the pockets was food that hadn't worked, and the food that was gone was the food that went to the race.

## New shoes, not race shoes

The same week had new shoes in it. They didn't get to race, they got to train, because the [shoe that gets the bib](/notes/the-shoe-you-trained-in) is the one that's already done a long run, and these hadn't done anything yet but sit in the box.

This brick was the big one. The two weeks after it had the Honolulu Triathlon and the Hibiscus Half in them, then Honu, which is three race weekends in a row, and the [next note](/notes/three-weekends-in-a-row) is about getting two bodies through that.
