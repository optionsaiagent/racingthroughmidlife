The Honolulu Marathon was December 10, 2023. This is the note about the week after it, which is a week nobody films because nothing happens in it. That's the point of it.

## What we did

Walked. Swam easy, mostly floating with intent. Slept more than usual and didn't apologize for it. Ate normally, which after a marathon means more than normal for a few days and then it settles. Didn't run.

The urge to run a few days after a marathon is real. The legs feel better than you'd expect by Wednesday and the brain says a short shakeout would be fine. At thirty, maybe. At fifty-one, the Wednesday shakeout is what makes the first real run in January feel like it's happening on somebody else's legs.

## Why the whole week

A marathon's a big withdrawal. The [season gets paid for in sleep](/lessons/sleep-funds-the-season), and a marathon spends most of the balance in one morning. The week after isn't a reward for finishing. It's the deposit that makes the next season possible.

We plan it ahead of time. The week after the marathon goes on the calendar as rest before the marathon goes on the calendar as a race. That way it can't get argued with in the moment by a version of us that feels surprisingly fine on Thursday.

## What came next

January. The [five pounds and the groove](/notes/january-the-five-pounds-and-the-groove), same as the year before. Then a New Year's Day 10K in Hawaii Kai, because the calendar here doesn't give you much of a break, and a trail run out to Kaena Point. Then the decision about the full distance, which was already being talked about in the car on the way home from the finish.

But that was next year. This week was for sitting down.
