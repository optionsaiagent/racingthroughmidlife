The first video we ever posted as a weekly log was about losing five holiday pounds. Not an inspiring opening, but an honest one, and if you're reading this in the first week of a January with a number on the scale you don't like, I've been exactly where you are.

## Where we were

The Honolulu Marathon was December 11, 2022. Jay's fiftieth birthday race, the one he never wanted, done. Then Christmas, then New Year's, then that specific kind of tired you get from finishing a big thing and immediately stopping.

The first week of January wasn't a training week. It was a week of getting the routine back, the alarm, the dark, the shoes by the door. Were the five pounds real? Yes. Were they the point? Not really, even if they felt like it at the time. The groove was the point. The weight follows the groove, it doesn't lead it, and I learned that one the slow way, okay, the very slow way.

## What "back in the groove" actually looked like

Short, easy, early. Nothing that would make anybody's highlight reel. A run that was mostly about being outside before the sun came up. A swim that was mostly about remembering how to swim. The plan for the year had a half marathon in it three weeks out, the [Akahai](/races/akahai-half-2023), and that wasn't a goal race. It was a reason to keep the long run alive through the holiday hangover, and I needed one.

## Why this note is here

Because every year has a January, every January feels like starting over, and the temptation is to make it dramatic. Big goals, big block, big first week. I've watched that fail in other people and I've watched it fail in me, more than once.

The version that worked for me was smaller, and it was boring. I got the alarm back. I got the first easy week done. I let the five pounds wait until the routine came back, as the routine was the part I could actually control. January was for finding the groove, not proving anything, and I didn't prove anything that week. I did get up.
