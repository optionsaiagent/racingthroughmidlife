I'll say it: most couples-training advice is a lie with a smile on it. You won't hold the same watts and you won't want the same long-run pace, and one of you will be ready to chat at mile eight while the other one is having a private argument with a hamstring. Some weeks I'm the one and some weeks I'm the other, and I'm not always sure which until mile eight.

We've been doing this since 2014 and seriously since 2022. This is what it actually looks like in our house.

## Weekdays apart

The short sessions during the week are separate. Different times, different routes, sometimes different sports. Jay might swim at dawn while I ride later, or the other way around. Trying to sync two weekday schedules around two jobs and two bodies is how both workouts get skipped, and apart, they both happen. I'm a routine person, and my routine works better when it isn't waiting on his.

## Weekends together

The long stuff is shared. The long run, the long ride, the brick. That's where the training relationship actually lives, and it's where [whose day is it](/lessons/whose-day-is-it) matters most.

In our house, the person whose race it is sets the pace and the other one sits on it, even if it feels easy, and especially if it feels easy. Hibiscus 2023 was my day. Jay's job that morning wasn't a Strava segment, it was a 2:12 that belonged to me, and he did the job.

## The kitchen isn't a third workout

Dinner doesn't turn into a second race. If the evening becomes a negotiation about macros or who ate what, the week's already gone. We eat, we talk about something that isn't the plan, and we go to bed early enough for the alarm. Boring? Yes, and I like it that way (predictable and routine-based, that's me).

## When the seasons don't line up

The hardest version of this is when one of us is tapering and the other one's building. Who wins? The taper wins the calendar, because the race is closer, and the person building does their long work alone that week. We say it out loud on Sunday night, as the unspoken version has failed us every single time we've tried it.

We'll keep writing this one down as the seasons change. The rule doesn't. Whose day is it.
