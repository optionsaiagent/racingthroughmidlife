I'll say it: most couples-training advice is a lie with a smile on it. You won't hold the same watts. You won't want the same long-run pace. One of you will be ready to chat at mile eight and the other one will be having a private argument with a hamstring. Some weeks I'm the one, some weeks I'm the other.

We've been doing this since 2014 and seriously since 2022. Here's what actually works in our house.

## Weekdays apart

The short sessions during the week are separate. Different times, different routes, sometimes different sports. Jay might swim at dawn while I ride later, or the other way around. Trying to sync two weekday schedules around two jobs and two bodies is how both workouts get skipped. Apart, they both happen. I'm a routine person, and my routine works better when it isn't waiting on his.

## Weekends together

The long stuff is shared. The long run, the long ride, the brick. That's where the training relationship actually lives, and it's where [whose day is it](/lessons/whose-day-is-it) matters most.

The person whose race it is sets the pace. The other one sits on it, even if it feels easy, especially if it feels easy. Hibiscus 2023 was my day. Jay's job that morning wasn't a Strava segment. It was a 2:12 that belonged to me.

## The kitchen isn't a third workout

Dinner doesn't turn into a second race. If the evening becomes a negotiation about macros or who ate what, the week's already gone. We eat, we talk about something that isn't the plan, and we go to bed early enough for the alarm. Boring? Yes. It works.

## When the seasons don't line up

The hardest version of this is when one of us is tapering and the other one's building. Who wins? The taper wins the calendar, because the race is closer, and the person building does their long work alone that week. We say it out loud Sunday night. The unspoken version fails every time.

We'll keep writing this one down as the seasons change. The rule doesn't. Whose day is it.
