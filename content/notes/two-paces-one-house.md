Most couples-training advice is a lie told with a smile. You will not hold the same wattage. You will not want the same long-run pace. One of you will be ready to talk at mile eight and the other will be conducting a private argument with a hamstring.

We have been doing this since 2014 and seriously since 2022. Here is what actually works in this house.

## Weekdays apart

The short sessions during the week are separate. Different times, different routes, sometimes different sports. Jay might swim at dawn while Michelle rides later, or the reverse. Trying to sync two weekday schedules around two jobs and two bodies is how both workouts get skipped. Apart, both happen.

## Weekends together

The long work is shared. The long run, the long ride, the brick. That is where the training relationship actually lives, and it is where the [whose day is it](/lessons/whose-day-is-it) rule matters most.

The person whose race it is sets the pace. The other person sits on it, even if it feels easy, especially if it feels easy. Hibiscus 2023 was Michelle's day. Jay's job was not a Strava segment. It was a 2:12 that belonged to her.

## The kitchen is not a third workout

The other thing that works: dinner does not become a second race. If the evening turns into a negotiation about macros or who ate what, the week is already lost. We eat, we talk about something that is not the plan, and we go to bed early enough for the alarm.

## When the seasons do not line up

The hardest version of this is when one of us is tapering and the other is building. The taper wins the calendar because the race is closer, and the person building does their long work alone that week. We say this out loud on Sunday night. The unspoken version fails every time.

We will keep writing this down as the seasons change. The rule stays. Whose day is it.
