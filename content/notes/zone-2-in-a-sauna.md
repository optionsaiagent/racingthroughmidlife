Zone 2 is the easy aerobic effort. The pace where you can talk in full sentences. Every endurance book says spend most of your training there, and in September 2023 we started actually doing it instead of nodding along.

The problem is where we live.

## Heat drift

In humidity, your heart rate climbs through a session even when the pace doesn't. Your body's working to cool itself and can't, so the heart works harder to push blood to the skin. An effort that starts in zone 2 at 5:30 a.m. is out of it by 7 without the pace changing at all.

So "run in zone 2" here means one of two things. Either you slow down as the morning warms up, which feels ridiculous when the pace is already slow, or you accept that the back half of the session isn't zone 2 anymore and plan for it.

We do both, depending on the day. Neither one's wrong. Pretending the drift isn't happening is wrong.

## What it looked like

The runs got slower. Slower than felt reasonable. Slow enough that the first few weeks were a small argument with the ego every morning. But the runs also got longer without leaving a mark, and when the hard sessions came, there was something behind them.

We put one hard bike session in that month, a VO2 max effort on the trainer, mostly to remember what hard felt like. One. The rest was easy, early, and long.

## Why this matters after 45

At this age the easy work is where the volume lives. The hard sessions cost more to recover from than they did at thirty, so there are fewer of them, and the base has to come from somewhere. It comes from a lot of boring mornings at a pace that would embarrass a twenty-year-old.

We're not going to give you zones or numbers. We don't know your heart. We'll tell you that once we started treating the easy days as actually easy, the heat quit winning every session.

The [trainer in the garage](/notes/the-trainer-in-the-garage) helped with this more than we expected, because on the trainer there's no drift from the sun, only from the fan not being big enough.
