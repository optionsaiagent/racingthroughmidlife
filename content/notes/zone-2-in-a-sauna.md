Zone 2 is the easy aerobic effort. The pace where you can talk in full sentences. Every endurance book says to spend most of your training there, and in September 2023 we started actually doing it instead of nodding at it.

The problem is where we live.

## Heat drift

In humidity, heart rate climbs through a session even when the pace does not. Your body is working to cool itself and it cannot, so the heart works harder to move blood to the skin. An effort that starts in zone 2 at 5:30 a.m. is out of it by 7 without the pace changing at all.

So "run in zone 2" here means one of two things. Either you slow down as the morning warms up, which feels absurd when the pace is already slow, or you accept that the second half of the session is not zone 2 anymore and plan for it.

We do both, depending on the day. Neither one is wrong. Pretending the drift is not happening is wrong.

## What it looked like

The runs got slower. Slower than felt reasonable. Slow enough that the first few weeks were a small argument with the ego every morning. But the runs also got longer without leaving a mark, and the hard sessions, when they came, had something behind them.

We put one hard bike session in that month, a VO2 max effort on the trainer, to remember what hard felt like. One. The rest was easy, early, and long.

## Why this matters after 45

At this age the easy work is where the volume lives. The hard sessions cost more to recover from than they did at thirty, so there are fewer of them, and the base has to come from somewhere. It comes from a lot of boring mornings at a pace that would embarrass a twenty-year-old.

We are not going to give you zones or numbers. We do not know your heart. We will tell you that once we started respecting the easy days as easy, the heat stopped winning every session.

The [trainer in the garage](/notes/the-trainer-in-the-garage) helped with this more than we expected, because on the trainer there is no heat drift from the sun, only the fan.
