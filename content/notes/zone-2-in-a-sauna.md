Zone 2 is the easy aerobic effort, the pace where you can talk in full sentences, which for an introvert was never the selling point. Every endurance book says spend most of your training there, and in September 2023 we started actually doing it instead of nodding along.

The problem is where we live.

## Heat drift

In humidity my heart rate climbs through a session even when the pace doesn't. The body's working to cool itself and can't, so the heart works harder to push blood to the skin. An effort that starts in zone 2 at 5:30 a.m. is out of it by 7 without the pace changing at all, and I've watched it happen on the watch more mornings than I can count.

So running in zone 2 here came down to one of two things for us. Either we slowed down as the morning warmed up, which felt ridiculous when the pace was already slow, or we accepted that the back half of the session wasn't zone 2 anymore and planned for it. We did both, depending on the day, and neither one felt wrong. Pretending the drift wasn't happening was the only version that didn't work.

## What it looked like

The runs got slower. Slower than felt reasonable. Slow, okay, embarrassingly slow, slow enough that the first few weeks were a small argument with my ego every single morning. Was I really going to run that slowly on purpose? I was, and I did, and I didn't love it. But the runs also got longer without leaving a mark, and when the hard sessions came, there was something behind them that hadn't been there before.

We put one hard bike session in that month, a VO2 max effort on the trainer, mostly to remember what hard felt like. One. The rest was easy, early, and long.

## Why this matters after 45

At this age the easy work is where the volume lives. The hard sessions cost more to recover from than they did at thirty, so there are fewer of them, and the base has to come from somewhere. For us it came from a lot of boring mornings at a pace that would embarrass a twenty-year-old. I'm fine with boring. Boring is my whole thing (routine-based, predictable, and happy about it).

Once we started treating the easy days as actually easy, the heat quit winning every session. It still won some.

The [trainer in the garage](/notes/the-trainer-in-the-garage) helped with this more than I expected, because on the trainer there's no drift from the sun. Only from the fan not being big enough.
