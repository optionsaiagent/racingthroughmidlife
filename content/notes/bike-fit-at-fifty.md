A bike fit is an hour or two with somebody who knows what they're doing, adjusting the bike to your body instead of the other way around. Saddle height, reach, cleat position, the angle of things you didn't know had angles.

Michelle had hers the third week of August 2023, in the middle of a season with a 112-mile ride and the Marathon Readiness Series in it. It should've happened a year earlier.

## Why it matters more now

A fifty-year-old body has opinions. A back that put up with a bad position at thirty doesn't anymore. A knee tracking slightly wrong on every pedal stroke will let you know about it somewhere around mile forty of a long ride, and it'll keep letting you know for the rest of the week.

A fit isn't about speed. It's about being able to ride long without paying for it on the run, or in the next week's training, or in a rib that was already unhappy.

## What changed

We're not going to list saddle millimeters. What we'll say is the long rides after the fit were different rides. Less shifting around on the saddle. Less of the hand numbness we'd been treating as normal. A position that could be held for hours, which is the entire job of a position when the ride's 100 miles.

## The order we'd recommend

Fit first, bike second. A fit on an old bike beats no fit on a new one. Jay's [tri bike showed up in July](/lessons/the-bike-came-after-the-race), and the honest version of that story is that both of us should've paid for a fit before either of us paid for a frame.

If you're starting this after 45 and you're going to spend money on one thing for the bike, spend it on the person who adjusts the bike to you.
